# Ideation frameworks reference

Use these frameworks selectively. Pick the lens that fits the idea. Do not run every framework mechanically.

- SCAMPER: transform an existing idea by substitute, combine, adapt, modify by magnify or minimize, put to other uses, eliminate, and reverse or rearrange. Best for improving existing products. Less useful for greenfield ideas.
- How Might We: reframe a pain point as How might we achieve an outcome for a specific user without a key constraint. Good framings are narrow enough to act on and broad enough to allow creative solutions, and they carry a tension that forces creativity. Best for breaking an anchor on a solution.
- First Principles Thinking: list what is known true, list every assumption, challenge each assumption that is convention rather than law, and rebuild from the truths. Best for breaking incremental-only thinking.
- Jobs To Be Done: name the functional job, the emotional job, and the social job in When plus I want plus so I can form. People hire products to do a job; the real competitor is the current workaround. Best when unsure the right problem is in view.
- Constraint-Based Ideation: impose time, feature, tech, cost, audience, or scale constraints to force creative solutions. Best for cutting an idea that grows too large or too vague.
- Pre-mortem: imagine the project shipped and failed, list every plausible technical, market, team, and timing cause, decide which are preventable and which would kill the project, and name what is accepted. Best for Phase 2 evaluation of ideas that feel good but lack pressure testing.
- Analogous Inspiration: look for structural similarities in other industries, products, natural systems, or historical precedent, not surface likeness. Best for Phase 1 expansion with variations that feel genuinely different.
