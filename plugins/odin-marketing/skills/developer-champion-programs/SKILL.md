# Developer champion programs

## Contract

| Field | Bound contract |
|---|---|
| Trigger | User asks for an advocate, champion, insider, or ambassador program design. |
| Authority | Read-only program design in chat. Consent-aware: opt-in ledger, permission for public recognition, no invented testimonials. No unsolicited contact, mandatory advocacy, content quotas, commission or MLM framing, forced referrals, automatic swag, or identity use without consent. |
| Side effect | A program design in chat. No one contacted or enrolled. |
| Done | Qualification signals, benefits, expectations, recognition rules, contributor routes, referral boundaries, and the opt-in ledger are present; every behavioral claim has evidence and every public use has a permission record. |

## Not for

- Public advocacy appearances; those belong to `developer-advocacy`. This skill owns the program structure and consent contract, which differ in input and output.

## Inputs

- `goal` (required): what the program exists to do.
- `evidence` (optional): behavioral signals already observed (contributors, helpers, writers) with sources. No signal is invented.
- `constraints` (optional): budget, staffing, and brand limits.

## Procedure

1. Define qualification signals from observed behavior only, with sources. **Done when:** each signal traces to evidence or is marked proposed-not-observed.
2. Set benefits proportional to contribution and capacity: access, recognition, learning, influence on roadmap input. **Done when:** each benefit names its cost and owner.
3. Set expectations without quotas: what champions may do, never must do. Refuse mandatory advocacy and content quotas explicitly. **Done when:** no quota or compulsion remains.
4. Write recognition rules: public use of a person name, quote, or likeness requires recorded permission; opt-out is immediate and complete. **Done when:** recognition rules include the permission and opt-out mechanics.
5. Draw referral boundaries: invited, never extracted; no commission framing that misleads; no forced referrals. **Done when:** referral rules are invitation-only and honest.
6. Open the opt-in ledger: who may enroll, how consent is recorded, how withdrawal works. **Done when:** enrollment, record, and withdrawal are all specified.

## Failure and recovery

- No observed behavioral evidence: design the pilot around proposed signals flagged as such; do not invent current champions.
- Budget or staffing insufficient: cut benefits to fit and name the cut.

## Output

A program design: goal, qualification signals with evidence, benefits with costs, expectations without quotas, recognition and permission rules, contributor routes, referral boundaries, and the opt-in ledger specification.
